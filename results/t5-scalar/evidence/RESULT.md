# T5: the scalar-congruence conjecture is a theorem

Date: 2026-07-23.
Problem: prove, for the q-deformed fundamental automorph
`Lambda_q = rho(Lambda) = [[A, B], [C, D]]` of `sqrt(d)`, that

> `C | B` if and only if `C | (A - D)` (divisibility in `Z[q, 1/q]`, up to monomials),

previously proved only at cyclotomic indices `n in {2,3,4,5}` as a byproduct of
proof (ii) of Lemma L.

**Outcome: full proof, via a new exact linear identity among the entries.**
The identity, which appears to be the all-orders strengthening of the known
first-jet identity `(A-D)'(1) = (d+1)s`, is

```
q (A - D)  =  (q - 1) (q B + C)          (exact, in Z[q, 1/q])
```

for every nonsquare `d`, and more generally for `rho(M)` of any
`M = [[a,b],[c,d]]` in `SL(2,Z)` with `c != 0` and `c | a - d`, in the form

```
(A - D) - (q-1) B  =  H_t C,     H_t := [t]_q + (q-1) q^(t-1),   t := (a-d)/c.
```

Since `q - 1` never divides `C` (because `C(1) = +-s != 0`), the identity gives
the conjecture in one line, together with a multiplicity-refined version and a
universal product formula for the two negation series (Corollaries below).

The congruence-subgroup specialization route suggested as the primary line of
attack was examined first; it is not what worked. What worked was scanning for
the exact local law factor by factor, discovering the identity empirically, and
then proving it by a palindrome induction in the free monoid on
`R = [[1,1],[0,1]]` and `L = [[1,0],[1,1]]`.

## (a) Counterexample scan, reported separately from the proof

All scans are exact integer or exact rational arithmetic (coefficient dicts or
`Fraction` long division). No floating point, no modular sampling. Code:
`scan_t5.py`, `cong_charact.py`, `verify_identity.py`, `general_law.py` in this
folder; data in `global_scan.csv`, `local_data.csv`, `control_scan.csv`.
`Lambda_q` was generated by `fastlam.lam_q_fast(d)` (fundamental +1 Pell
solution from `qcore.pell_plus1`, word from `proof_verification.sl2_word`,
exact product of `R_q` powers and `S_q`).

Populations covered, and results:

1. **Direct double divisibility test.** Every nonsquare `d` in `[2, 300]`
   (283 values, 140 with `C | A-D`, 143 without). Both divisibilities tested
   independently over `Q[q]`. Mismatches of the iff: **zero**.
2. **Local factor-by-factor test.** Every nonsquare `d` in `[2, 120]`,
   `C` factored into irreducibles over `Z`; 282 factor instances. With
   `(k, a, b) = (mult of phi in C, in A-D, in B)`, the only patterns observed:
   `(1,0,0)`, `(1,1,1)`, `(1,2,1)`, `(2,1,1)` and cyclotomic/finite variants,
   always satisfying `min(a,k) = min(b,k)`. Violations of the local iff:
   **zero** in 282.
3. **Congruence scan.** `C | (A-D) - (q-1)B` tested exactly on every nonsquare
   `d` in `[2, 1500]` (1462 values): failures **zero**.
4. **Exact identity scan (strongest).** `q(A-D) - (q-1)(qB+C) = 0` tested as an
   exact polynomial identity on every nonsquare `d` in `[2, 6000]`
   (5923 values): failures **zero**, with the plus sign strictly, no global
   minus anywhere in the range; by linear homogeneity in the entries the sign
   cannot depend on the representative, and the proof below fixes it to plus.
5. **Control populations.** Automorphs of `a + sqrt(d)` (91 matrices): the
   identity and iff hold. Random positive words in `PSL_q(2,Z)` (about 600 with
   `C != 0`): the congruence holds in a word **iff** the classical condition
   `c | a - d` holds, in every case tested (584 of 584); the general-`t` exact
   identity was confirmed on 1156 word instances with `c | a - d`, zero
   failures. Random signed words show that the gcd law is **not** universal in
   `PSL_q(2,Z)` (7 failures in 399), so the hypothesis `c | a - d` is genuinely
   load-bearing.

Coverage statement. These are facts about exactly the populations listed:
nonsquare `d <= 6000` for the identity, `d <= 300` for the direct double
divisibility, `d <= 120` for the local factor data, plus the word samples
described. Nothing was scanned beyond `d = 6000`. The scan outcome is not the
evidence for the general claim; the proof below is, and the scan serves as an
independent check of the proof and of the code conventions.

## (b) The q-free element check

`R_q S_q = [[q,1],[0,1]] [[0,-1/q],[1,0]] = [[1, -1],[1, 0]]`, verified
directly by `rho_fast` (`A=1, B=-1, C=1, D=0`), identical and free of `q`.

Formalization consistency: `C = 1` divides everything, so both sides of the
conjectured iff hold trivially. The check is not vacuous against the exact
identity: here `a=1, d=0, c=1`, so `t = 1`, and indeed
`(A-D) - (q-1)B = 1 + (q-1) = q = ([1]_q + (q-1)q^0) * 1 = H_1 C`, an exact and
nontrivial instance of the general identity. The formalization survives the
cheapest falsifier.

## (c) The proof

### Conventions

`rho : PSL(2,Z) -> PSL_q(2,Z)` with `R_q = [[q, 1],[0, 1]]`,
`S_q = [[0, -1/q],[1, 0]]`, as in the paper. `L = S R^{-1} S^{-1}`, so for
every integer `k`

```
R_q^k = [[q^k, [k]_q], [0, 1]],        L_q^k = [[1, 0], [q^(1-k) [k]_q, q^(-k)]],
```

with `[k]_q = (q^k - 1)/(q - 1)` in `Z[q, 1/q]` (also for `k <= 0`).
Representatives of `rho(M)` differ by global units `+-q^j`.

**Definition.** A matrix `X = [[A,B],[C,D]]` over `Z[q,1/q]` satisfies property
(P) if `q(A - D) = (q - 1)(q B + C)`. Property (P) is linear and homogeneous in
the entries, so it depends only on the projective class of `X`; for
`M in PSL(2,Z)`, "M satisfies (P)" is therefore well defined through any
representative of `rho(M)`. Equivalently, (P) says
`E := (A - D) - (q-1)B = (q-1) q^(-1) C`.

### Lemma 1 (bases)

`I`, `S`, `R^k`, `L^k` satisfy (P) for every integer `k`.

Proof. `I`: `0 = 0`. `S_q`: `A - D = 0` and `qB + C = q(-1/q) + 1 = 0`.
`R_q^k`: `q(q^k - 1) = (q-1) q [k]_q`. `L_q^k`:
`q(1 - q^(-k)) = (q-1) q^(1-k) [k]_q`. Both are the identity
`(q-1)[k]_q = q^k - 1`.

### Lemma 2 (symmetries)

`X` satisfies (P) iff `-X` does, iff `adj(X) = [[D,-B],[-C,A]]` does, iff
`S_q X S_q^{-1} = [[D, -C/q], [-qB, A]]` does.

Proof. Negation: both sides of (P) are linear. Adjugate: the claim for
`adj(X)` reads `q(D - A) = (q-1)(-qB - C)`, the negative of (P). Conjugation:
`S_q X = [[-C/q, -D/q],[A, B]]` and `S_q^{-1} = [[0,1],[-q,0]]` give
`S_q X S_q^{-1} = [[D, -C/q],[-qB, A]]`; the claim for it reads
`q(D - A) = (q-1)(q(-C/q) + (-qB)) = -(q-1)(C + qB)`, again the negative of
(P). Since `adj` and `S`-conjugation represent `rho(M^{-1})` and
`rho(SMS^{-1})` up to units, (P) is stable under inverse and `S`-conjugation.

### Lemma 3 (matched-ends extension)

If `X` satisfies (P), then so do `R_q^k X R_q^k` and `L_q^k X L_q^k`, for every
integer `k`.

Proof for `R`. Write `[k] = [k]_q`. The entries of `X' = R_q^k X R_q^k` are

```
A' = q^(2k) A + q^k [k] C
B' = q^k [k] A + q^k B + [k]^2 C + [k] D
C' = q^k C
D' = [k] C + D .
```

Then

```
q(A' - D') = q^(2k+1) A + q^(k+1)[k] C - q[k] C - q D ,
(q-1)(qB' + C') = (q-1) ( q^(k+1)[k] A + q^(k+1) B + q [k]^2 C + q [k] D + q^k C ).
```

Substitute the hypothesis in the form
`(q-1) q^(k+1) B = q^k ( q(A - D) - (q-1) C )` and use
`(q-1)[k] = q^k - 1` throughout:

* A-terms: `(q^(2k+1) - q^(k+1)) A + q^(k+1) A = q^(2k+1) A`, matching.
* D-terms: `-q^(k+1) D + q(q^k - 1) D = -q D`, matching.
* C-terms: `-q^k (q-1) C + q [k](q^k - 1) C + (q-1) q^k C = q^(k+1)[k] C - q[k] C`,
  matching.

So `X'` satisfies (P). The `L` case follows formally:
`S (R^k Y R^k) S^{-1} = L^{-k} (S Y S^{-1}) L^{-k}` since `S R S^{-1} = L^{-1}`,
so the `L`-statement for exponent `-k` is the `S`-conjugate of the
`R`-statement (Lemma 2), and `k` ranges over all integers. (The `L` case was
also verified by the same direct expansion, symbolically for `k <= 4`;
see `verify_identity.py`, block v2.)

### Lemma 4 (positive words and palindromes)

(i) Every `M in SL(2,Z)` with all entries `>= 0` is a product of `R` and `L`
in exactly one way (the nonnegative matrices form a free monoid on `R, L`).

(ii) If in addition `M = [[a,b],[c,a]]` has equal diagonal and `c != 0`, then
its `R,L` word, written in blocks `Z_1^(e_1) ... Z_n^(e_n)` with alternating
letters, is a palindrome (`e_i = e_(n+1-i)`, same letters at mirrored places)
with an odd number of blocks.

Proof. (i) Standard descent. If `c = 0` then `ad = 1` and `M = R^b`; if
`b = 0` then `M = L^c`. If all entries are `>= 1`: `a > c` and `d > b`
simultaneously would give `1 = ad - bc >= (c+1)(b+1) - bc = b + c + 1`,
impossible; `a < c` and `d < b` simultaneously would give
`1 = ad - bc <= (c-1)(b-1) - bc = 1 - b - c`, impossible. So either
`a >= c, b >= d` (then `M = R M'` with `M' = R^{-1} M` nonnegative) or
`a <= c, b <= d` (then `M = L M'`), the entry sum strictly drops, and ties
(`a = c` or `b = d`) force `det` considerations that make the letter unique.
Induction terminates at `I`.

(ii) For 2 by 2 matrices, `J M^T J = [[d, b],[c, a]]` with `J = [[0,1],[1,0]]`,
so equal diagonal is exactly `J M^T J = M`. On words, transposition reverses
the product and swaps `R^T = L`, while `J`-conjugation swaps `J R J = L`,
`J L J = R`; composing, `J M^T J` is the word of `M` written backwards with
the same letters. By uniqueness (i), the word equals its reversal. In an
alternating block palindrome the mirrored blocks carry equal letters, and
block `i` has the same letter as block `n+1-i` only if `n` is odd.

### Proposition 5 (equal diagonal)

Every `M = [[a,b],[c,a]] in SL(2,Z)` satisfies (P).

Proof. If `c = 0` or `b = 0` or `a = 0`: from `a^2 - bc = 1` these are
`+-R^b`, `+-L^c`, `+-S^(+-1)`, covered by Lemmas 1 and 2. Otherwise
`bc = a^2 - 1 > 0`, so `b` and `c` have the same sign, and `|a| >= 2`.
Replacing `M` by `-M` (Lemma 2), assume `a >= 2`. If `b, c < 0`, replace `M`
by `S M S^{-1} = [[a, -c],[-b, a]]` (Lemma 2), which is positive. So assume
all entries positive. By Lemma 4 the word of `M` is an alternating block
palindrome with an odd number of blocks `2m + 1`. Induct on `m`: for `m = 0`,
`M` is a single block `R^e` or `L^e` (Lemma 1); for `m >= 1`, peel the matched
outer blocks, `M = Z^e M' Z^e` with `M'` again an alternating block palindrome
with `2m - 1` blocks, and apply Lemma 3. (The inductive statement is (P) for
all alternating block palindromes; equal diagonal is not needed inside the
induction.)

### Theorem 6 (general form)

Let `M = [[a,b],[c,d]] in SL(2,Z)` with `c != 0` and `c | a - d`, and set
`t := (a - d)/c`. Then, for every representative of `rho(M)`,

```
(A - D) - (q - 1) B  =  H_t C,      H_t = [t]_q + (q-1) q^(t-1)  ( = [t+1]_q - q^(t-1) ).
```

Proof. First, `c | a^2 - 1`: from `d = a - tc` and `ad - bc = 1`,
`a^2 - 1 = c(b + ta)`. So `M_0 := [[a, (a^2-1)/c], [c, a]]` is in `SL(2,Z)`,
has equal diagonal, and a direct check gives `M = M_0 R^(-t)`.

Right multiplication law: for `X' = X R_q^u`,

```
A' = A q^u,  B' = A[u] + B,  C' = C q^u,  D' = C[u] + D,
```

so, using `q^u - (q-1)[u] = 1`,

```
E(X R^u) = E(X) - [u]_q C(X),        C(X R^u) = q^u C(X).
```

By Proposition 5, `E(M_0) = H_0 C(M_0)` with `H_0 = (q-1)q^(-1)` (that is (P)
rewritten). Hence with `u = -t`:

```
E(M) = E(M_0) - [-t] C(M_0) = ( H_0 + q^(-t)[t] ) C(M_0) = q^(-t) H_t C(M_0) = H_t C(M),
```

using `[-t] = -q^(-t)[t]` and `q^(-t)H_t = q^(-t)[t] + (q-1)q^(-1)`. Both
sides are linear homogeneous in the entries, so the identity holds for every
representative.

### Theorem 7 (the scalar-congruence conjecture)

Let `d` be nonsquare, `Lambda = [[r, ds],[s, r]]` the fundamental automorph,
`Lambda_q = [[A,B],[C,D]]`. Then

```
q (A - D) = (q - 1)(q B + C),
```

and consequently `C | A - D` if and only if `C | B`, in `Z[q, 1/q]` up to
monomials; when either side holds, `Lambda_q == A * I (mod C)`.

Proof. `Lambda` has equal diagonal (`t = 0`) and `s != 0`, so Theorem 6 gives
`(A - D) - (q-1)B = (q-1)q^(-1) C`, the displayed identity. Thus
`A - D == (q-1) B (mod C)`. The element `q - 1` is prime in `Q[q, 1/q]` and
does not divide `C`, because `C(1) = +-s != 0`. Hence over `Q[q,1/q]`,
`C | A - D  iff  C | (q-1)B  iff  C | B`. Integrality: all identities are in
`Z[q,1/q]`; if `C | A - D` in `Z[q,1/q]` then `(q-1) B / C` is in `Z[q,1/q]`,
and a Laurent polynomial whose product with `q - 1` has integer coefficients
has integer coefficients (its coefficients are partial sums of the integer
ones). The converse direction is immediate from the identity. When both hold,
`A == D` and `B == 0 (mod C)`, so `Lambda_q == A * I (mod C)`.

### Corollary 8 (multiplicity refinement)

For every irreducible `phi` in `Q[q]`, `phi != q`:
`min(v_phi(A - D), v_phi(C)) = min(v_phi(B), v_phi(C))`; equivalently
`gcd(C, A - D) = gcd(C, B)` up to units. (For `phi | C` one has
`phi != q - 1`, so `v_phi((q-1)B) = v_phi(B)`, and
`v_phi((A-D) - (q-1)B) >= v_phi(C)` forces the two minima to agree; for
`phi` not dividing `C` both minima are `v_phi(C) = 0`.) This matches the local
scan table exactly, including the `(k, a, b) = (2, 1, 1)` pattern at `d = 53`.

### Corollary 9 (universal norm formula)

For every nonsquare `d`, with `x = [sqrt(d)]_q` and `y = [-sqrt(d)]_q`,

```
( 1 + (q-1) x ) ( 1 + (q-1) y )  =  (q^2 - q + 1) / q .
```

Proof. By the Pell reduction, `x` and `y` are the two fixed points of the
Mobius action of `Lambda_q`, hence the roots of `C w^2 - (A-D) w - B`, so
`x + y = (A-D)/C` and `x y = -B/C`. Theorem 7 divided by `qC` reads
`x + y = (q-1)(-xy) + (q-1)/q`, that is `F_d + (q-1) Pi_d = (q-1)/q` where
`F_d` is the negation sum and `Pi_d := x y` the negation product. Expanding
the left side of the display gives `1 + (q-1)^2/q = (q^2 - q + 1)/q`.

Hand checks: `d = 2` gives `Pi_2 = -(q^2+1)/q^2`, so `-B/C = (q^2+1)/q^2`,
value `2 = d` at `q = 1`; `d = 7` gives
`-B/C = (q^4 + 2q^3 + q^2 + 2q + 1)/q^3`, value `7` at `q = 1`. Both agree
with the known finite `F_2`, `F_7`.

The formula makes the analytic content of T5 transparent: the sum `F_d` and
the product `Pi_d` of the two negation series satisfy one linear relation with
unit coefficients, so one is a Laurent polynomial exactly when the other is.
The finite class is exactly the class where the q-deformed Pell form has both
its coefficient ratios Laurent.

### Remark (fixed point at q-infinity)

The identity is equivalent to the vector identity

```
Lambda_q (1, 1-q)^T  =  D (1, 1-q)^T + C S_q (1, 1-q)^T ,
```

and `(1, 1-q)` represents `1/(1-q) = lim [n]_q`, the q-deformation of the
point at infinity. At every root `z0` of `C`, counted with multiplicity, the
specialized Mobius map `Lambda_q(z0)` fixes the point `1/(1-z0)`. The vector
`(1, 1-q)` is an exact eigenvector of `R_q` (translation invariance of
q-infinity), which is why the statement is stable under the moves
`M -> R^t M R^u` and depends only on the fraction `a/c`; classically the
condition `c | a - d` is `a^2 == 1 (mod c)`, a property of `a/c` alone.

### Remark (first jet)

Differentiating `q(A-D) = (q-1)(qB + C)` at `q = 1` gives
`(A-D)'(1) = B(1) + C(1) = +-(d+1)s`, recovering the known first-derivative
identity used in proof (i) of Lemma L; the identity is its all-orders
strengthening. It also forces the second-jet relation `g_2 = (m-1) g_1`
through the entry duality, consistent with the jets subsection of the paper.

### What the theorem does and does not subsume

It proves item 3 of the open ledger in full, at every index and every
irreducible factor of `C`, with multiplicities (Corollary 8), for every
nonsquare `d`. It does not re-prove Lemma L: the unconditional vanishing
`Phi_n | A - D` for `Phi_n | C`, `n in {2,3,4,5}` or prime, still needs the
congruence-subgroup or pi-adic arguments; the new theorem converts each such
vanishing into the paired vanishing of `B` and vice versa. It says nothing new
about which `d` are finite (Conjecture cyclo untouched).

### Honesty and open edges

* The converse empirical characterization, that for a general positive word
  the congruence `C | (A-D) - (q-1)B` holds only if `c | a - d`, was observed
  in 584 of 584 sampled words but is not proved here and is not needed for T5.
* The identity is elementary enough that it could exist in the literature on
  q-deformed rationals (left-right deformations, `[infinity]_q = 1/(1-q)`
  appears in MGO). The T5 literature shortlist (LMG 2101.02953,
  BRY 2603.08439, KMRWY 2403.08446, RY 2502.02974) was not exhaustively
  searched in this session. A corpus check is recommended before claiming
  novelty in print; the proof itself is self-contained either way.
* Conventions: everything is stated for `rho` with the `R_q, S_q` used
  throughout the project and by `fastlam.py`; property (P) is projective, so
  representative choices cannot affect any statement.
